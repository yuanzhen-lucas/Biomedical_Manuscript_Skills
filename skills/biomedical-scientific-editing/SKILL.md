# Scientific editing workflow

## When to use
Use for line editing, clarity revision, terminology consistency, or structure improvement of scientific text.

## When not to use
Do not use to strengthen claims, introduce evidence, create a missing section from assumptions, or conceal substantive scientific edits as grammar changes.

## Required inputs
Source text and requested editing scope.

## Optional inputs
Target journal/style guide, preferred terminology, glossary, claim matrix, and tracked-change format.

## Workflow
1. Mark unresolved scientific or data gaps before editing.
2. Preserve figures, numbers, units, denominators, citations, and uncertainty qualifiers.
3. Classify edits as grammar, style, clarity, structure, logical, statistical, methodological, evidence, or claim-strength.
4. Separate substantive changes from copyedits and explain rationale.
5. Recheck edited text against original and claim-evidence links.

## Dependencies
Claim-audit and original-article/narrative-review workflow as applicable.

## Scientific safeguards
“Associated with” does not become “caused”; “not significant” does not become “no effect”; animal findings do not become human efficacy.

## Failure behavior
Retain ambiguous passages with an author query rather than resolving scientific ambiguity by rewriting.

## Output contract
Revised text, categorized changes, unresolved questions, and a statement of scientific meaning preserved or author-verified changes.

## Verification steps
Compare all numbers/citations/claim strength and uncertainty before and after editing.

## Human-review requirements
Authors approve changes that affect meaning, interpretation, or attribution.
