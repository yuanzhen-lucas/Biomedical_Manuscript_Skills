# Narrative review workflow

## When to use
Use for non-systematic review planning, thematic synthesis, and narrative review drafting.

## When not to use
Use the systematic-review skill when the work claims systematic methods or needs protocol-driven screening and PRISMA flow reporting.

## Required inputs
Review topic/scope, intended audience, and available sources or search scope.

## Optional inputs
Date/language limits, study matrix, mechanisms, clinical relevance, and journal style.

## Workflow
1. Define scope and describe how sources were identified, including deliberate limitations.
2. Map evidence by design, population/model, outcome, direction, replication, and directness.
3. Synthesize themes, conflicting evidence, method differences, and open questions.
4. Draft claims with traceable sources and calibrated language.
5. Audit for selective citation and overgeneralization.

## Dependencies
Literature-search, paper-reader, evidence-synthesis, claim-audit.

## Scientific safeguards
Never use “systematic review” as a stylistic label. Do not treat citations as a substitute for evidence comparison.

## Failure behavior
State where source selection was selective or incomplete; do not manufacture comprehensiveness.

## Output contract
Scope statement, evidence map, thematic draft/outline, contradictory findings, limitations, and gaps.

## Verification steps
Check each substantive claim and ensure important contradictory or null findings are represented.

## Human-review requirements
Authors confirm review scope, source selection, mechanistic interpretation, and conclusions.
