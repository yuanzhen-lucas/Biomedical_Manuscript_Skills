# Evidence synthesis workflow

## When to use
Use when comparing multiple studies or creating a narrative cross-study synthesis.

## When not to use
Do not use to label a review systematic unless its protocol, search, eligibility, screening, extraction, and synthesis methods support that designation.

## Required inputs
Research question and a set of identified sources with known access/verification depth.

## Optional inputs
Study design, populations, interventions/exposures, outcomes, estimates, appraisal assessments, certainty framework, and search log.

## Workflow
1. Create one row per study/report and link related reports of the same study.
2. Compare design, population, exposure, outcome, result direction, precision, directness, and limitations.
3. Identify convergence, contradiction, heterogeneity, replication, and missing domains.
4. Keep evidence quality/appraisal separate from identity verification and reporting completeness.
5. Draft synthesis organized by evidence dimensions rather than paper order.

## Dependencies
Paper-reader and reference-manager records; optional claim matrix.

## Scientific safeguards
Do not resolve contradictory evidence by voting or by selecting fluent sources. Avoid single-study generalization and preserve negative results.

## Failure behavior
If evidence is too sparse or heterogeneous, state that thematic or quantitative synthesis is not justified.

## Output contract
Evidence matrix, synthesis themes, counterevidence, limitations, research gaps, and claim-level source links.

## Verification steps
Trace each synthesis sentence to the included studies and re-check study-level estimates and population/model scope.

## Human-review requirements
Domain experts assess directness, bias, certainty, and whether a synthesis conclusion is justified.
