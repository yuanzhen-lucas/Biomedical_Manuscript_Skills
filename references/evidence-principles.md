# Evidence principles

- A reference's bibliographic identity and whether it supports a claim are different questions.
- Verification depth (L0–L4) describes what was checked. It is not risk of bias, certainty, or evidence quality.
- Source records should retain design, population/model, exposure/intervention, comparator, outcome, result, uncertainty, location, and limitations when reported.
- Abstract-only material cannot support full-text verification claims.
- Contradictory evidence should be represented explicitly, not averaged away.
- A source's evidence may be indirect for the target population, model, intervention, outcome, or setting.
- Negative or inconclusive evidence should remain visible. P > 0.05 is not proof of no effect.
- Search result snippets are discovery metadata, not extracted evidence.

These principles are implemented as model fields and workflow safeguards, not as a validated automated evidence-grading instrument.
