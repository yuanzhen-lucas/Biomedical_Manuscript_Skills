# Study-design interpretation notes

Record the design as reported by study authors, then assess whether that label matches the methods. Relevant families include randomized and nonrandomized interventions, cohort/case-control/cross-sectional studies, diagnostic accuracy, prognostic/prediction models, qualitative research, case reports/series, laboratory and animal experiments, and evidence syntheses.

The design constrains interpretation but does not by itself settle causal validity. Some observational analyses may support causal estimates under explicit assumptions and suitable design/analysis. Conversely, a randomized label does not prove adequate allocation, concealment, follow-up, or analysis.

Always retain model level: in vitro, organoid, animal, human observational, or human interventional. Do not silently promote preclinical findings to human efficacy.

For a particular study, use the appropriate design-specific reporting guidance and separate reporting completeness from methodological appraisal.
